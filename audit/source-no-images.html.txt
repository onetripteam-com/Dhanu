<div class="narration"><span class="mini-label">A VERY BAD EXPLANATION</span>“A possible tomorrow,” the stranger said. “One of many. I boarded this ship a long time ago. I wanted to know every danger before I chose a direction.”</div>
<div class="line-quote">“And?” Dhanu asked.<br/><br/>“I’m still here.”</div>
<div class="narration small">Nix landed on the ship’s rail. “Lovely reunion. Shall I put the two of you down as one passenger or two disappointments?”</div>
<div class="narration">A cold wind moved over the deck. The city behind them disappeared into fog. Ahead, a hundred ships floated without crews — some magnificent, some barely more than broken wood. Every one was perfectly still.</div>
<div class="panel panel-full ch2-panel ghost-panel"><img alt="Dozens of empty ghostlike sailing ships lie scattered across luminous turquoise water and mist." loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED svg+xml 27238 chars]"/><span class="panel-mark">PANEL 12 · THE SEA OF ALMOSTS</span><div class="panel-copy">A graveyard of journeys<br/><strong>that never began.</strong></div></div>
<div class="narration small">There were ships that never left because they waited for better weather. Others had turned back at the first storm. One had a perfectly polished hull and no sails at all.</div>
<div class="line-quote">“That one,” Nix said, nodding toward the polished ship, “spent twelve years picking a name.”</div>
<div class="narration">The stranger unrolled a map. It was blank except for three tiny ink marks. “Manifesto lies beyond this sea. When the storm comes, follow the brightest beacon. It always looks safe.”</div>
<div class="narration small">Dhanu noticed the word <em>looks.</em> The stranger noticed him noticing.</div>
<div class="quiet-beat"><span>THE WIND DIED.</span><span>THEN THE SEA STOOD UP.</span></div>

<div class="narration small">A wave tore across the deck. Dhanu grabbed the railing. The stranger had already retreated below, clutching the useless map.</div>
<div class="line-quote">“Good news,” Nix shouted over the wind. “This is a very educational way to drown.”</div>
<div class="narration">Then a radio, rusted into the captain’s desk, crackled to life. That made no sense. There was no electricity. There was barely a tomorrow.</div>
<div class="panel panel-medium ch2-panel radio-panel"><img alt="A worn brass radio glows with an impossible signal, its dial illuminated in the dark cabin." loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED svg+xml 3174 chars]"/><span class="panel-mark">PANEL 14 · THE IMPOSSIBLE SIGNAL</span><div class="panel-copy">A voice came through<br/><strong>the static.</strong></div></div>
<div aria-label="A short signal from Dhanu’s best friend, Sanjay" class="radio-transcript"><img alt="An illustrated portrait of Sanjay, the friend sending Dhanu a radio clue." class="sanjay-transmission" loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED webp 52851 chars]"/><div class="radio-top"><span class="radio-led"></span> INCOMING TRANSMISSION <span>00:00 / UNKNOWN</span></div>

<p><strong>SANJAY:</strong> “Bro, questions later. You need an answer? First make sure something can answer back.”</p>
<p class="radio-noise">— SIGNAL LOST —</p></div>
<div class="narration small">Dhanu tried the dial again. Nothing. The signal had lasted only a few seconds, but it was enough to make the whole impossible ocean feel a little less empty.</div>
<div class="narration">Nix tapped the ship’s bell with his beak. “Your friend is annoyingly sensible. Shall we see which light can hear us?”</div>

<div class="narration"><span class="mini-label">THE FIRST LIGHT / THE ISLAND ABOVE THE CLOUDS</span>By the time Dhanu reached the staircase, the sea had vanished under a sheet of mist. <div class="panel panel-full character-scene temple-panel"><img alt="A mountain temple rises above layers of mist as dawn lights the stone steps." class="scene-bg" loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED svg+xml 5942 chars]"/><img alt="Dhanu in his navy expedition jacket and red scarf, ready to climb." class="scene-character dhanu-ascent" loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED webp 58395 chars]"/><span class="panel-mark">PANEL 16 · A STAIRCASE WITHOUT AN END</span><div class="panel-copy">The stairs climbed into<br/><strong>an unfinished morning.</strong></div></div>
<div class="narration small">The next step said TWO. Then THREE. Then FOUR. Somewhere above the mist, pale sunlight reached the mountain, though midnight had never arrived below. Dhanu looked up. There were far too many steps to count.</div>
<div class="line-quote">“How high is this temple?” Dhanu asked.<br/><br/>“Depends,” said Nix. “How good are you at regretting your decisions?”</div>
<div class="narration">Halfway up, he found another number painted into the stone: <strong>999.</strong> Beyond it, a landing. An open gate. And a courtyard worn smooth by the same pair of feet moving along the same line.</div>
<div class="panel panel-medium ch3-panel"><img alt="A battered training courtyard beneath moonlight and falling rain, its floor worn by repeated practice." loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED svg+xml 31386 chars]"/><span class="panel-mark">PANEL 17 · THE COURTYARD</span><div class="panel-copy">A thousand paths,<br/><strong>worn into one.</strong></div></div>
<div class="narration small">An old bellkeeper stood in the courtyard, practising with a plain wooden staff. Step. Turn. Strike. Return. No flourish. No audience. Each movement ended exactly where the next one began.</div>
<div class="dialogue-strip"><div class="strip-voice"><small>DHANU</small>“Are you training for something?”</div><div class="strip-voice alt"><small>THE BELLKEEPER</small>“I was. Then I realised I was training for every day after that.”</div></div>
<div class="narration">The bellkeeper lowered the staff. His hands were rough. His sleeves had been repaired so often that almost none of the original cloth remained. Behind him hung an enormous bronze bell.</div>
<div class="narration small">There were 999 tally marks cut into the wall. The last was fresh.</div>
<div class="panel panel-full ch3-panel"><img alt="An enormous bronze temple bell hangs in shadow, held by worn beams and an iron brace." loading="lazy" src="[EMBEDDED svg+xml 4170 chars]"/><span class="panel-mark">PANEL 18 · THE SILENT BELL</span><div class="panel-copy">Nine hundred and ninety-nine strikes.<br/><strong>Not one sound.</strong></div></div>
<div class="narration">“That bell has been silent for 999 mornings,” the bellkeeper said. “The second piece of Manifesto rests inside it. Ring it, and it's yours.”</div>
<div class="line-quote">“That's it?”<br/><br/>“That's everything.”</div>
<div class="narration small">Dhanu took the wooden striker. He swung. The bronze shuddered. No note followed — only a dull thud. He swung harder. A second thud. Nix winced.</div>
<div aria-hidden="true" class="comic-sfx"><span>THUD.</span><span>...THUD.</span></div>
<div class="narration">“You can keep striking,” said the bellkeeper. “Or you can tell me what changed between the first and the second.”</div>
<div class="narration small">Dhanu examined the bell. A thick iron brace was pressed against its side. Above it hung a counterweight, pulled just slightly out of line. Someone had been trying to ring a bell that could barely move.</div>
<div class="line-quote">“Did you know it was jammed?” Dhanu asked.<br/><br/>The bellkeeper looked at his own 999 marks.<br/><br/>“I needed a thousand mornings to learn to look properly.”</div>
